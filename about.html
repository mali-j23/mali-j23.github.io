About

I came to epidemiology through fieldwork. During Pakistan's 2022 floods I entered disease reports from affected districts into DHIS2 at the National Institute of Health in Islamabad. The next year I collected household data on barriers to immunization in urban slum communities, interviewing families in Urdu, Punjabi, and Hindi.

I was part of the first BS Public Health cohort at Bahria University in Islamabad. My thesis surveyed 120 healthcare practitioners about telemedicine and found the barrier was knowledge, not willingness. I graduated in 2024 with a 3.9 GPA and moved to Connecticut in January 2025 for a Master of Public Health, with a graduate certificate in epidemiology, at the University of New Haven.

The MPH gave me the methods, and two internship sessions at the Connecticut Department of Public Health let me use them on real state data. In spring 2026 I analyzed the 2024 BRFSS in SAS and found a steep income gradient in heart disease. In summer 2026 I used CDC WONDER and Joinpoint regression to show that Connecticut's ischemic stroke death rate was rising more than twice as fast as the nation's.

The thread through all of it is the same: who carries the burden, and what the data can and can't see. The BRFSS result that adults with a doctor report more heart disease is a good example. It's a sign of who gets diagnosed, which means the people with the least access are undercounted.

Now I'm a Research and Advocacy Fellow at UNH's WeEmbody Lab and serve on SOPHE's national Advocacy Committee. I finish my MPH in December 2026 and I'm looking for epidemiology, public health analyst, and research roles. I'm open to relocating anywhere in the U.S.

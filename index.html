A result worth reading carefully

In the 2024 BRFSS, adults earning under $25K had about twice the odds of reported heart disease compared with those earning $75K or more. Smoking and obesity raised the odds too.

The largest odds ratio belongs to having a personal doctor (3.2). That's a sign of who gets diagnosed. Adults without regular care are likely undercounted, so the real income gap is probably wider than the survey shows.

Stroke death rates in Connecticut vs. the U.S., 2018–2024

Connecticut Department of Public Health, summer 2026

Age-adjusted mortality from CDC WONDER, split by stroke subtype and tested for trend changes with Joinpoint regression.

Connecticut's ischemic stroke death rate rose 15.4% per year, more than double the national 6.7%. Hemorrhagic stroke was not the driver.

Used: CDC WONDER, ICD-10 coding, age adjustment, Joinpoint (NCI)

Heart disease and its socioeconomic gradient in Connecticut

Connecticut Department of Public Health, spring 2026

Cleaned and recoded the 2024 Connecticut BRFSS in SAS, then estimated weighted prevalence and design-adjusted odds ratios.

Adults earning under $25K had about twice the odds of reported heart disease (OR 2.19) compared with those earning $75K or more.

Used: SAS, PROC SURVEYFREQ, PROC SURVEYLOGISTIC, BRFSS weights

How practitioners in Islamabad and Rawalpindi see telemedicine

Undergraduate thesis, Bahria University, 2024

A cross-sectional survey of 120 healthcare practitioners, built on the Technology Acceptance Model and analyzed in SPSS.

Three in four were positive about telemedicine, but only one in four scored as having good knowledge of it. Training, not attitude, was the gap.

Used: survey design, SPSS, Technology Acceptance Model, Diffusion of Innovation

How healthcare practitioners in Islamabad and Rawalpindi see telemedicine

A cross-sectional survey of 120 practitioners on what they know about telemedicine, what they think of it, and whether they would use it.

Study summary

Question
What do healthcare practitioners in Islamabad and Rawalpindi know about telemedicine, how do they perceive it, and how willing are they to adopt it?
Why it matters
Telemedicine depends on the clinicians who would use it. Knowing whether the barrier is knowledge or attitude tells training and policy where to focus.
Data and population
Cross-sectional survey of 120 healthcare practitioners in the twin cities, including PIMS and KRL Hospital, selected by purposive sampling.
My role
I designed the study and questionnaire, collected and analyzed the data, and wrote and defended the thesis, supervised by Dr. Sidra Shahid.
Methods
Questionnaire built on the Technology Acceptance Model and Diffusion of Innovation theory. Knowledge, perception and willingness scales, each classed as good or high at 60% or more. Descriptive analysis in SPSS.
Main finding
76.0% had a positive perception and 77.7% high willingness to adopt telemedicine, but 75.2% scored as having poor knowledge of it.
What it may mean
Openness outpaced knowledge, which points to practitioner training as a lever for adoption. The study describes the gap; it doesn't test what predicts willingness.
Limitations
Purposive sampling in two cities limits generalizability. Responses are self-reported, and the analysis is descriptive.

Who took part

What I found

  • 86.0% agreed telemedicine is a feasible way to deliver care, and 76.0% agreed it could be integrated into the existing health system.
  • The gap between attitude and knowledge suggests training, rather than persuasion, is where adoption efforts should start.

Defense

I defended the thesis before a faculty committee with invited external examiners from other universities. It has not been published.

Arsenic in drinking water and ischemic stroke in rural Bangladesh

A policy brief and a study protocol for my environmental epidemiology course. The protocol is a proposed design, not a completed study.

Course
PUBH 6630 Environmental Epidemiology, UNH
When
May 2026
Instructor

Output
Policy brief and technical protocol

Part A: the policy brief

An estimated 57 million people in Bangladesh drink water with arsenic above the WHO guideline of 10 μg/L. The contamination traces back to tube well programs of the 1970s and 1980s that were meant to reduce waterborne disease. I synthesized the cardiovascular evidence and argued that WHO SEARO should require stroke surveillance in arsenic-endemic districts.

Evidence I drew on
StudyFinding
HEALS cohort, Araihazar (Wu et al. 2015)Stroke aHR 1.19 (95% CI 1.02 to 1.40) per 1-SD (101.3 μg/L) increase in well arsenic
Same cohort, gene interactionArsenic with ICAM1 rs281432: joint aHR 1.85 (1.14 to 3.01) for stroke
Dose-response meta-analysis (Moon et al. 2017)10 to 20 μg/L: RR 1.11 (1.05 to 1.17) for coronary heart disease, 1.09 (1.03 to 1.14) for CVD
REGARDS (Tsinovoi et al. 2018)Higher urinary MMA associated with nearly twice the ischemic stroke risk

The brief also answered two methodological objections: that lifestyle confounding explains the association, and that pooling stroke subtypes without neuroimaging dilutes it.

Part B: the study protocol

A proposed case-cohort study nested in the HEALS cohort (about 11,700 adults in Araihazar) on whether chronic arsenic exposure raises the 15-year incidence of first ischemic stroke.

  • Exposure: time-weighted mean well arsenic across visits, plus urinary arsenic speciation (MMA%, DMA/MMA) in the subcohort and cases.
  • Measurement error: treated as Berkson-type, with regression calibration as a prespecified sensitivity analysis.
  • Primary model: Prentice-weighted Cox regression with DAG-derived covariates, restricted cubic splines to test the dose-response shape, and Fine-Gray models for competing risks.
  • Spatial plan: empirical Bayes smoothed incidence, kriged arsenic surfaces, Moran's I and LISA cluster detection, and buffer analysis around high-arsenic wells.
  • Bias checks: a negative-control outcome, imaging-confirmed sensitivity analysis, and explicit discussion of survivorship bias.

Why it's here

It connects to my stroke mortality work at CTDPH, and it shows how I think about study design, confounding, and exposure measurement before any data is collected.

Arsenic in drinking water and ischemic stroke in rural Bangladesh

A policy brief and a study protocol. The protocol is a proposed design, not a completed study.

Study summary

Question
Does chronic arsenic exposure from tube well water raise the incidence of first ischemic stroke among adults in Araihazar, Bangladesh?
Why it matters
An estimated 57 million people in Bangladesh drink water above the WHO arsenic guideline of 10 μg/L, and the contamination traces back to tube well programs of the 1970s and 1980s.
Data and population
Proposed: a case-cohort study nested in the HEALS cohort (about 11,700 adults in Araihazar). The brief synthesizes published cohort and meta-analytic evidence.
My role
Individual final project for Dr. Roheeni Saxena's course, May 2026.
Methods
Evidence synthesis, then a protocol: time-weighted arsenic exposure, Prentice-weighted Cox models, restricted cubic splines, Fine-Gray competing risks, and a spatial plan.
Main point
The published evidence links arsenic to cardiovascular disease and ischemic stroke specifically; the protocol is designed to test that within one well-characterized cohort.
Limitations
No new data were collected or analyzed. The brief relies on published estimates.

Evidence

Table 1. Evidence I drew on

Same cohort, gene interactionArsenic with ICAM1 rs281432: joint aHR 1.85 (1.14 to 3.01) for stroke
Dose-response meta-analysis (Moon et al. 2017)10 to 20 μg/L: RR 1.11 (1.05 to 1.17) for coronary heart disease, 1.09 (1.03 to 1.14) for CVD
REGARDS (Tsinovoi et al. 2018)Higher urinary MMA associated with nearly twice the ischemic stroke risk

The brief argued that WHO SEARO should require stroke surveillance in arsenic-endemic districts, and answered two objections: that lifestyle confounding explains the association, and that pooling stroke subtypes without imaging dilutes it.

Protocol design

  • Exposure: time-weighted mean well arsenic across visits, plus urinary arsenic speciation (MMA%, DMA/MMA) in the subcohort and cases.
  • Measurement error: treated as Berkson-type, with regression calibration as a prespecified sensitivity analysis.
  • Primary model: Prentice-weighted Cox regression with DAG-derived covariates, splines for dose-response shape, and Fine-Gray models for competing risks.
  • Spatial plan: empirical Bayes smoothed incidence, kriged arsenic surfaces, Moran's I and LISA cluster detection, and buffer analysis around high-arsenic wells.
  • Bias checks: a negative-control outcome, an imaging-confirmed sensitivity analysis, and explicit treatment of survivorship bias.
